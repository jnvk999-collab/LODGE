# Krishna Grand — how to actually fill the rooms

The website and the reception system are the easy half. In a tier-3 town they
are maybe 30% of what fills rooms. This document is the other 70%.

Read this before spending a single rupee on advertising.

---

## 1. Who sleeps in a Kadapa lodge

Kadapa has five distinct demand pools. Most lodges serve only the last one,
which is why most lodges fight on price.

| Segment | Stay length | Why they pick you | How hard to reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hospital attendants** | 3–10 nights | Weekly rate, hot water any hour, flexible checkout | Easy — nobody else is targeting them |
| **Corporate / contractor** | 2–4 weeks | Monthly rate, GST invoice, single bill | Easy — direct approach works |
| **Pilgrims** | 1–2 nights | Near the dargah, early breakfast, safe parking | Medium — via travel agents |
| **Exam candidates** | 1 night | Known exam dates, wake-up call, early breakfast | Medium — predictable calendar |
| **Walk-in / transit** | 1 night | Whoever is nearest with a vacancy | Easy, but lowest margin and zero loyalty |

Chinna Chauku is mid-town, which suits the first two best. **Target hospital
attendants and contractors first.** They stay longest, they do not haggle
nightly, and almost nobody markets to them.

---

## 2. The order of operations

Do these in order. Do not skip to step 4.

### Step 1 — Google Business Profile (free, highest return)

This is roughly 80% of the return of everything in this document.

People in Kadapa search *"lodge near me"*, *"lodges in kadapa"*, *"hotels near
kadapa bus stand"* on their phone and call the top three results. Most lodges
in town have no listing, or one with two blurry photos and no phone number.

- Create the profile standing **at the lodge gate**, so the pin is right.
  The nearby *Care dental* pin is ~50 m off — do not reuse it.
- Category: *Lodging* (add *Budget hotel* as secondary).
- 20+ photos: bathroom, mattress, AC unit, parking, reception, stairwell.
  Not just the building front. People book on the bathroom photo.
- Hours: open 24 hours.
- Add the WhatsApp number and the website link.
- Turn on messaging and actually answer it.

**Then get reviews.** Print a small card for the reception desk with a QR
code to your review link. Ask every guest who looked happy at checkout.
Target 100+ reviews at 4.3★ or better. This single thing beats any paid
advertising available to you.

### Step 2 — WhatsApp Business (free)

In a tier-3 town people call or WhatsApp. They do not fill in web forms.

- Install **WhatsApp Business**, not regular WhatsApp.
- Catalogue: one entry per room type with a photo and the price.
- Greeting message with tariff and directions.
- Saved replies for the five questions you will answer fifty times a day.
- UPI QR for advance payment.
- **Somebody must answer it at 11 PM.** An unanswered WhatsApp is a booking
  that went to the lodge down the road.

The website's booking form opens WhatsApp with the guest's details already
typed in, so every web enquiry lands here.

### Step 3 — The commission network (this is how tier-3 actually works)

Unglamorous, and it will out-perform your website in year one.

- **Auto and taxi drivers** at the railway station and RTC bus stand.
  ₹50–100 per guest delivered. Pay reliably and immediately — word travels
  between drivers faster than any advertisement.
- **Hospital front desk and duty staff** at RIMS and the private hospitals.
  Attendant families ask them where to stay. Leave cards.
- **Function hall managers** — wedding guest overflow, booked in blocks.
- **Travel agents** running Tirupati and Ahobilam packages.
- **The advocate's office in your own building** and the shops on the street.
  They are asked for directions daily.

Print 500 cards. Distribute them yourself, not through staff — people
remember the owner's face.

### Step 4 — OTAs (MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, then Booking.com)

Only after steps 1–3 are running.

Yes, 15–20% commission hurts. Take it for the first 6–12 months anyway,
because OTAs bring two things you cannot buy: **occupancy while you are
unknown**, and **reviews**. The reviews are the real asset.

Then convert. Put a card in every room: *"Book direct next time — 10%
cheaper. Save this number."* A repeat guest booking direct costs you nothing.

### Step 5 — Only now, paid advertising

By this point you will know from the reception system's **Reports → Where
your business comes from** table which channels actually produce revenue.
Spend there. Not before.

## 3. Cheap things that lift ratings

Budget-hotel reviews are almost entirely about five things. Get them right
and 4.5★ is reachable without spending much.

- **Hot water, 24 hours.** The single most common complaint in budget lodge
  reviews anywhere in India.
- **24-hour checkout, not 11 AM.** Trains into Kadapa arrive at awkward
  hours. This is a genuine differentiator and costs nothing on a day when
  the room would sit empty anyway.
- **Free breakfast.** Tie up with a nearby tiffin centre at ~₹25/head. The
  biggest single lever on OTA rating.
- **WiFi that works.** Rare enough in this segment that it gets mentioned.
- **A bathroom that photographs well.** Then photograph it.

Add: safe parking inside the compound, a luggage room, and a monthly rate
advertised on a board at reception.

## 4. Pricing

Do not compete on the base rate. Vary by date instead.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Thursdays and Urs at Ameen Peer Dargah | +30–50% |
| APPSC / TET / bank / railway exam weekends | +30–50%, and take bookings 3 weeks early |
| Wedding season | +30%, sell family rooms in blocks |
| Normal weekday | Base rate |
| Long stay (7+ nights) | 25–30% below base — you are buying occupancy certainty |

Most lodges in town charge one flat rate all year. That leaves serious money
on the table on maybe 40 nights a year and prices you too high on the other
325.

Track the exam calendar and the dargah calendar. Put them on a wall
calendar at reception.

---

## 5. What to measure

Open **Reports** in the reception system once a week. Three numbers matter:

- **Occupancy %** — how full you are.
- **ADR** (average daily rate) — what a sold room earns on average.
- **RevPAR** (revenue per available room) — occupancy × ADR. **This is the
  real score.** Raising occupancy by discounting can lower RevPAR; that is a
  bad deal that looks like a good month.

Then read **Where your business comes from**. Within three months that table
tells you exactly which of the channels above is worth your time. It is the
reason the reception system asks "Booking came from" at every check-in — do
not let staff skip that field.

---

## 6. First 90 days

**Weeks 1–2 (before opening).** Google Business Profile live with 20 photos.
WhatsApp Business set up. Website live. 500 cards printed. Exam and festival
calendar on the reception wall.

**Weeks 3–6.** Walk the commission network yourself — drivers, hospitals,
function halls, travel agents. Ask every departing guest for a Google review.
Target 25 reviews by week 6.

**Weeks 7–10.** List on MakeMyTrip and Goibibo. Approach three construction
or infrastructure firms with a written monthly corporate rate.

**Weeks 11–13.** Read the Reports tab. Double down on whichever channel is
producing. Drop whatever is not.

---

## 7. Before you open

Confirm with a local CA or consultant — not from this document:

- GST registration and the correct slab for your tariff band
- Trade licence from the municipal corporation
- Guest register and police reporting requirements
- Fire safety clearance
- Signage permissions

Getting these wrong is expensive and entirely avoidable.
